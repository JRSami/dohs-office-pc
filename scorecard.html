How the scoring works

Read this before you trust the ranking

Price is computed, everything else is judgement. The price score comes straight from the desk total — cheapest machine scores 10, dearest scores 2, everything else falls on the line between. The other seven scores are mine, assigned from the specifications and from what the Dhaka market looks like this month. They are defensible, not objective. If you disagree with one, the honest thing is to say so rather than let the arithmetic launder the opinion.

The desk total is what you actually pay. Towers and mini PCs carry ৳17,000 of monitor, UPS and peripherals on top of the box. All-in-ones carry ৳6,700 because the screen is built in. Laptops carry ৳10,300 — external monitor and keyboard, but no UPS, because the battery is the UPS. That is why a laptop that looks dear next to a tower closes some of the gap, and why the all-in-one is less silly than its sticker suggests.

A weight of zero removes an attribute entirely. If warranty genuinely does not matter to you, slide it to zero and watch what happens — the refurbished EliteBook climbs several places. That movement is the point of the tool: it shows you which of your priorities is actually driving the answer.

Availability is the volatile one. Several of these machines were listed but unbuyable when I checked. That score will go stale faster than any other, so ring the shop before you let it decide anything.
