What you are trading away

This is the lowest-cost configuration that still meets the specification worth insisting on: 16 GB fitted at the factory, a 500 GB NVMe drive, a proper monitor, a UPS and a legitimate software licence. What it does not have is a fast processor. The Intel N95 is four cores at 15 W — untroubled by Word, email, browsing and everyday spreadsheets, and visibly slower once a large workbook, a database front-end and a dozen browser tabs are open together. Spending ৳19,000 more on the Lania DX2 i5 buys a desktop-class six-core chip and nothing else you need. If this desk is heavy accounts work, that is money well spent; if it is general office work, it is not. Drop to the 256 GB version and the total falls another ৳3,000.

Read this before you compare prices

RAM and SSD prices are abnormally high right now

Memory and flash have been in a sustained price spike, and Bangladesh retail reflects it sharply. At Star Tech every cheap 16 GB stick is out of stock — Crucial at ৳4,100, BORY at ৳3,800, both unavailable — while what you can actually buy today is G.Skill Aegis at ৳13,200, Kingston FURY at ৳18,300, and laptop-format SO-DIMMs at ৳18,600. A 512 GB NVMe drive runs ৳8,500. That inverts the usual advice in two ways.

Assembling a fresh Core i3-14100 box today — CPU ৳18,000, H610 board ৳11,000, 16 GB ৳13,200, 512 GB SSD ৳8,500, casing and PSU roughly ৳4,500 — lands near ৳55,000 with no monitor. A Walton Core i5-10400 pre-built with SSD, HDD, casing and local warranty is ৳48,550 complete.

For pure office work the pre-built wins on price, on warranty, and on the thing that actually matters when a machine dies on a Tuesday: one shop to go back to. Assembling only makes sense if you specifically want current-generation parts and the freedom to upgrade later.

Spec floor

16 GB RAM is the one place not to economise — and right now you want it fitted at the factory, not added later. A staffer with Excel, Outlook, Chrome with fifteen tabs and a database front-end open will hit the wall on 8 GB inside a year. But adding a stick yourself today costs ৳13,200–18,600 at spot prices, while a machine sold with 16 GB already in it was built with memory bought before the spike. Pay for the 16 GB configuration up front; it is usually the cheapest 8 GB you will ever buy. Finding one is harder than it sounds — there is a table further down listing every 16 GB pre-built I could find in Dhaka, and it is a short list.

On the Walton machines: the 10th-gen Intel chips are old, but for Word, Excel and a browser they are more than sufficient, and Walton's service network across Dhaka is the practical advantage over an imported brand. The weakness is the storage — 256 GB SSD paired with a mechanical 1 TB drive, where the Qbits box gives you 500 GB of NVMe outright.

Not all mini PCs are the same machine. The distinction that matters is the chip class, and it is easy to miss because both come in the same small box. The MeLE and most cheap mini PCs use Intel's N-series — N95, N100, N150 — low-power chips that run Office fine but stall once a large workbook and a database client are open together. The Lania DX2 runs the same desktop Core i3-12100 and i5-12400 as the towers above. Same silicon, same performance, smaller box.

The AMD APU towers — faster chips, bigger electricity bill

Between ৳27,000 and ৳35,000 the market offers a genuinely tempting alternative: full-size towers built on AMD's APUs — the Ryzen 3 3200G, Ryzen 5 3400G and Ryzen 5 5600G. On processor alone they beat the N95 comfortably. The 3200G and 3400G are four-core parts with more headroom than the N95; the 5600G is a six-core, twelve-thread Zen 3 chip that outruns almost everything else in this guide at ৳34,450.

The catch is what they cost to run. These are 65 W processors in full-size cases with mechanical hard drives, against a 15 W chip in a fanless box. Expect roughly 45–50 W more at the wall through a working day. Over eight hours a day, twenty-six days a month, that is about 10 kWh — at the commercial off-peak rate of ৳13.82 per unit, near ৳135 a month, or close to ৳5,000 over three years. That is a sixth of the machine's purchase price handed quietly to the utility, and it does not show up in any price comparison.

There is a second cost that matters more in Dhaka than the money. The ৳3,750 Apollo 650 VA unit that carries a 15 W mini PC for hours will carry a 65 W tower for a fraction as long — and a tower really wants a larger, dearer UPS to begin with. During load-shedding that is the difference between working through the cut and saving your file and stopping.

Availability is better than it first appears. Star Tech's older listings for the Ryzen 5 3400G at ৳27,100 and the 5600G at ৳34,450 are both marked discontinued, but three current builds are in stock: the Ryzen 3 2200G at ৳30,000, the Ryzen 5 2400G at ৳33,499, and a Ryzen 5 3400G gaming build at ৳36,799. Walton's Ryzen 3 3200G tower is listed at ৳29,000 through TechLand but is out of stock, as is every other Walton tower on their site. None of the Star Tech listings state memory, so assume 8 GB and ask.

Worth knowing about the chips themselves: the 2200G and 2400G are 2018 parts, the 3400G a 2019 one. They are real desktop cores and they will outrun an N95, but they are two processor generations behind the 12th-gen Intel builds at similar money — and every one of them is 65 W. The 3400G build is badged a gaming PC, which usually means a larger power supply and sometimes a discrete graphics card, making it the thirstiest machine in this guide and the one with the shortest UPS runtime.

Where that leaves them: if raw processor speed is what you need and the desk runs something genuinely demanding, the 5600G is the best chip per taka anywhere on this page — worth chasing down at another retailer. For ordinary office work it is the wrong trade: you pay more to buy, more to run, and get less UPS protection, in exchange for performance the workload never asks for.

The Qbits Lania DX2, specifically

Qbits is a Bangladeshi company based at Mirpur Shopping Centre in Dhaka, and the DX2 is the machine they market as the first user-upgradable mini PC built here. For the desk you are describing it lines up unusually well:

  • ✓Dual SO-DIMM slots, user-accessible, and a 16 GB factory configuration exists. Given today's memory prices, order that config rather than upgrading later.
  • ✓500 GB NVMe as standard, expandable to 2 TB — roughly double what the Walton and Eastern IT towers ship with.
  • ✓Three-year warranty, carry-in, from a company headquartered in Dhaka. Longer than most towers in this price band.
  • ✓1.36 kg, 195 mm square, aluminium. Low draw means the ৳3,750 Apollo UPS runs it far longer than it runs a full tower — a real advantage during load-shedding.

Three things to weigh against that. Distribution is thin: neither Star Tech, Ryans, TechLand nor BDStall carries it — you buy from Qbits directly, Pickaboo, Daraz or Tofa. Availability is also unreliable — the DX2 has shown out of stock on Daraz, and marketplace listings for it stay up with correct pricing whether or not the unit can actually be bought. And a three-year warranty is only worth what the company behind it is worth — Qbits is young and much smaller than Walton, so weigh that on a five-year horizon.

Where the DX2 i5 sits in this guide: it is the upgrade, not the default. At roughly ৳47,000 against the Mini F's ৳36,999 you are paying about ৳10,000 for six desktop cores in place of four low-power ones — worth it for a heavy accounts or inventory desk, hard to justify for general office work. Its stock position is also the weakest of the three. If you need several units delivered on a schedule and the budget allows, the Walton i5 is the dependable tower alternative.

The one comparison worth pausing on: if the disputed Pickaboo price holds, the DX2 with a desktop Core i3-12100 is ৳25,999 — ৳2,000 less than the Mini F at 16 GB/500 GB, with a considerably better chip. What the N95 actually is. Four cores, four threads, no hyperthreading, 15W. It handles Word, email, browsing and modest spreadsheets without complaint. It slows down visibly when a large workbook, a database front-end and a dozen browser tabs are all open — which is exactly the desk you described. There is also a hardware ceiling worth knowing: the Mini F has a single SO-DIMM slot where the DX2 has two, so whatever memory you order is what you have unless you replace the stick outright.

Where it is the right machine: a reception desk, a data-entry or billing terminal, a shop-floor or warehouse PC, a shared browsing machine, or a second unit for someone who mostly reads email. At 15W it draws so little that the ৳3,750 Apollo UPS will run it for hours rather than minutes — for a counter that must stay working through load-shedding, that is a real argument. Mounted behind the monitor it also frees the whole desk.

At Qbits' own price this is a narrower call than it first appeared. ৳36,999 for the 16 GB / 500 GB version still buys factory-fitted memory, a real NVMe drive and a three-year warranty on one invoice, in stock — but it is no longer the cheapest route to those things. Pickaboo's ৳27,999 listing, if honoured, restores the original argument completely. Two things to be clear-eyed about before committing. The N95 is the one part that cannot be upgraded later, so if this desk grows into heavy spreadsheet work you replace the machine rather than improve it. And the single RAM slot means 16 GB is what you have; going further means discarding the stick you paid for. Buy it knowing both, and it is a sound, cheap, honest office PC.

The 8 GB / 128 GB version is the one to rule out entirely. Qbits does not publish its price; going by the ৳2,000 step between storage tiers elsewhere in the range, expect somewhere near ৳21,000 — but the number is not the problem. Windows 11 Pro plus Office and a year of updates will occupy 60–80 GB of a 128 GB drive, and Windows needs roughly 20 GB free to install a feature update at all. That leaves almost nothing for the OneDrive sync that your Business Basic licence is paying for. You would spend the machine's life managing free space. Combined with a single RAM slot holding 8 GB, it is the cheapest configuration Qbits sells and the worst fit for this desk.

The Lania X1: Core i7-1165G7

The X1 is Qbits' portable model and it matters here for one reason — it comes in exactly the 16 GB / 500 GB configuration you want, at ৳46,999, which is within a few thousand taka of the DX2 i5. If the DX2 cannot be supplied, this is the substitute that keeps the specification intact.

The chip is an 11th-gen Core i7-1165G7: 4 cores, 8 threads, 2.8–4.7 GHz, 28W, with Iris Xe graphics. Note what that is and isn't. It is a mobile i7, and against the DX2's desktop Core i5-12400 — 6 cores, 12 threads, a full desktop power budget — it loses on sustained multi-core work, which is exactly what a large recalculation is. The "i7" badge is doing more work than the silicon. You are paying about ৳4,000 more than the DX2 i5 for less processor.

There is also a cheaper X1 — a Core i3-1115G4 with 8 GB and 256 GB at ৳29,999, sold from Qbits directly. At Qbits' pricing it now sits ৳7,000 below the Mini F, which makes it a serious alternative rather than a curiosity, so it is worth setting the two side by side.

Against that ৳2,000 the X1 i3 gives you Thunderbolt 4, DisplayPort, and a chip that clocks to 4.1 GHz — it will open a single document noticeably faster. What it takes away is more substantial: the i3-1115G4 is two physical cores where the N95 has four, and it ships with half the memory and half the storage. Two cores is the thinnest processor in this entire guide, and 8 GB is below the floor this page argues for, with SO-DIMM memory at ৳18,600 to close the gap. On the balance of what an accounts desk actually does — many things open at once — four cores and 16 GB beat two cores and 8 GB, and the Mini F is the cheaper machine besides. Take the X1 i3 only if Thunderbolt connectivity is genuinely what you are after.

What the i7 gives you in return is materially better connectivity: USB-C 4.0 with Thunderbolt 4, plus HDMI and DisplayPort 1.2, against the DX2's HDMI 1.4 and VGA. If a second monitor is ever likely — and for an accounts desk it is one of the better productivity purchases going — the X1 drives two properly. It also takes up to 64 GB of RAM against the DX2's 32.

Check the warranty on this one

Qbits' own site states a three-year limited warranty across the Lania range, but the Daraz listing for the X1 says two years — and the Mini F product pages carry badges promising a lifetime servicing warranty and a 45-day replacement guarantee, which is a third set of terms again. That is a meaningful difference on a machine costing ৳47,000, and it is the kind of discrepancy that only gets settled in writing. Ask for the warranty term on the invoice before you pay.

The UPS is the item people cut first and regret. A desktop loses whatever is unsaved the instant the power drops, and repeated abrupt shutdowns are one of the more common causes of SSD failure and Windows corruption. At ৳3,750 the Apollo is under 9% of the hardware budget and protects the other 91%.

Size it against the machine, not the price. The 650 VA / 300 W rating is generous for the recommended setup — a 15 W mini PC and a 20 W monitor together draw under a fifth of what it can supply, which means a long, comfortable runtime. Put a 65 W AMD tower on the same unit and you are running near a third of capacity with correspondingly less time on battery. This is the load-shedding version of the electricity argument: the efficient machine is not just cheaper to run, it stays running longer on the same battery.

The chosen route: Business Basic at ৳4,440 a year

At ৳370 a month this is the cheapest fully legitimate way to put a staff member on business email at your own domain, with 1 TB of OneDrive behind them. Against Business Standard it saves ৳34,680 over three years — real money for a small business, and close to half the cost of the machine itself.

It comes with one condition attached, and it is the whole condition: Business Basic licenses the web and mobile Office apps only. No desktop Excel, no desktop Word, and — the part people are most often caught by — no desktop Outlook either. Microsoft's licensing documentation is explicit that Business Basic does not entitle a user to the new Outlook for Windows. Mail is read in a browser.

That is entirely workable, but only if you close the gap deliberately rather than discovering it in week two. Two free additions do it:

  • 1Install ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors for real spreadsheet work. Free, and closer to Excel's formats and interface than LibreOffice. Files still live in OneDrive, so nothing about the Microsoft side changes — the staff member just gets a proper desktop application for the big workbooks instead of a browser tab.
  • 2Decide how mail gets read. Outlook on the web is fine and needs no setup, but it needs a live connection — which, during a load-shedding afternoon on mobile data, is exactly when it is least convenient. Free Thunderbird connects to a Microsoft 365 mailbox and keeps mail readable offline. Set it up on day one, not after the first outage.

The one scenario where this choice bites is Excel macros and Power Query. ONLYOFFICE does not run VBA reliably and does not support Power Query at all, and neither does Excel for the web. If any workbook this desk depends on has macros in it, test that workbook before you commit — that is the trigger to move up to Business Standard, and it is worth knowing now rather than in March.

You can change your mind cheaply. Upgrading a Business Basic seat to Business Standard is a plan change on the same account and the same mailbox — no migration, no new email address, nothing to reinstall. So starting on Basic costs you nothing in flexibility. Add Microsoft 365 Apps for business instead if you want desktop Office but not the extra collaboration tools.

One cost that isn't on the table: the domain. Business email at yourbusiness.com needs a domain you own. Budget around ৳1,199 a year for a .com or ৳2,500 for .com.bd, from a local registrar that takes bKash. Buy the domain before you buy the Microsoft licence — setting up the mailbox means pointing DNS records at Microsoft, and doing that in the right order saves an afternoon.

Worth saying plainly

Unlicensed Windows and Office are common in Dhaka and cost nothing up front. They also mean no security updates on the machine holding your accounts, activation that breaks at inconvenient moments, and exposure if a client or a partner ever audits you. With Business Basic at ৳370 a month, the legitimate path is now cheap enough that the risk is not worth taking.

You asked specifically

Can you just run Linux?

Technically, yes, and it saves real money: no Windows licence (৳20,200) and no Office licence. Linux Mint or Ubuntu LTS on a Core i3 with 16 GB is fast, stable and free, and ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors — which is closer to Microsoft's file formats and interface than LibreOffice — handles .docx and .xlsx well enough that most office documents move back and forth without visible damage.

But for a small business in Dhaka there are four specific things that break it, and you should check all four before committing:

  • 1Accounting software. Tally, Troyee, and most locally-written accounting and inventory packages are Windows-only. If your books live in one of these, Linux is off the table for that desk. This is the single most common dealbreaker.
  • 2Excel macros and Power Query. ONLYOFFICE and LibreOffice do not run VBA macros reliably and do not support Power Query at all. If any workbook you rely on has macros in it, test them before switching.
  • 3Bank and NBR portals. Most now work in a browser, but some digital-signature and token utilities for VAT filing and corporate banking still ship Windows-only drivers. Verify with your bank first.
  • 4Support. Almost no Dhaka retail shop will troubleshoot Linux, and your staff member will need retraining. If you have no in-house technical person, factor in the cost of your own time.

My read: Linux is a good fit if the desk is doing documents, spreadsheets, email and browsing with no Windows-only software behind it — a reception, admin or content role. For an accounts or inventory desk in a Bangladeshi small business, the odds that something in the workflow is Windows-only are high enough that the savings are not worth the disruption. Middle path worth considering: run Windows on the machine, but use free ONLYOFFICE instead of paid Office, and pay only for email. That captures most of the saving with none of the compatibility risk.

At the shop

Checks before you pay

  • ✓Buy the 16 GB configuration, don't upgrade later. At today's memory prices a factory-fitted 16 GB is far cheaper than 8 GB plus a stick. If only 8 GB is available, ask whether it is a single module and whether a slot is free — 2×4 GB in a two-slot machine means throwing both away.
  • ✓For the Qbits Lania, confirm stock and lead time in writing before committing, and ask which service point handles carry-in warranty. It is not stocked by Star Tech or Ryans, so there is no walk-in fallback.
  • ✓Ask what OS it ships with. "Windows installed" at a Dhaka shop often means an unlicensed copy. Ask explicitly for a licence key or a DOS/no-OS machine you'll license yourself.
  • ✓Get the warranty terms in writing on the invoice — duration, and whether it is carry-in or on-site. Walton and the brand towers differ here.
  • ✓Check the monitor's cable. Some budget monitors ship VGA-only while the tower is HDMI-only. Confirm the cable is in the box.
  • ✓Test the UPS under load before leaving, and ask about the battery warranty specifically — it is usually shorter than the unit warranty.
  • ✓Buy the whole set from one shop if you can. When something fails you want one invoice and one counter to return to, not three.
  • ✓Before the machine arrives: register the domain, buy the Business Basic seat from a Bangladeshi Microsoft reseller, and point the DNS records at Microsoft. Then the staff member's first morning is spent working, not waiting for mail to start flowing.
  • ✓On day one: install ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors and set OneDrive to sync the working folder. The sync is the backup — with Business Basic you get 1 TB of it, and it costs nothing extra to switch on.

The spread between shops on identical items is real and worth ten minutes — the same HP M22F monitor was ৳15,200 at Ryans against ৳16,000 at Star Tech when first checked. Prices also move on their own: Star Tech's i3-12100 build rose ৳2,199 in two days. Qbits sells direct from Mirpur Shopping Centre and through Pickaboo, Daraz and Tofa.
